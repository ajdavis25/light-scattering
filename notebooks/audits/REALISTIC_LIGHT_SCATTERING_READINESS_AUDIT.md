## 1. Executive Summary

**Overall grade: 70 / 100 (C-)**
**Readiness verdict: Implementation is not yet ready; substantial technical and scientific validation work remains.**

This is a genuinely substantial piece of software — a from-scratch, spherically-stratified, polarized (Stokes I/Q/U/V), wavelength-resolved Monte Carlo radiative-transfer solver in C++ (`monte_carlo_cpp/`), with deterministic control variates, a real benchmark/measurement validation framework, and a disciplined publication-packaging layer (`paper/`). The core physics kernels that were inspected line-by-line are textbook-correct. That is real, creditable work.

However, the central claim the project currently rests on — that the flagship Marseille twilight comparison "passes" — is not scientific validation. It is a mathematical tautology, provably so, traced to exact source code. The five most important findings:

1. **The flagship "calibrated" Marseille validation result cannot fail, by construction, regardless of whether the physics is right or wrong.** The row-wise calibration solves `intensity_gain = reference / model`, `dolp_scale = reference / model`, and `aop_offset = reference − model` independently at each of the 683 sky directions, then re-applies those same three per-point factors to those same 683 points when scoring the gate (`monte_carlo_cpp/tools/build_measurement_model_calibration.py:88-100`, `monte_carlo_cpp/src/ValidationQA.cpp:317-334`). This was verified three independent ways during this audit (builder formula, C++ application formula, and a fresh row-by-row self-consistency check against all 683 rows — zero mismatches). The reported "normalized_rmse = 5.45e-18" is not evidence of physical correctness; it is floating-point round-trip noise. The project's own documentation is aware the result is "calibrated, not raw" — but nowhere does it state the sharper and more consequential fact: the specific headline numbers have **zero statistical power** to distinguish a correct model from a wrong one.
2. **The raw (pre-calibration) Marseille mismatch, extracted from the calibration CSV itself, is large and currently unexplained**: model intensity is off from the measured reference by roughly **9 orders of magnitude**, and that ratio varies by a factor of ~50× across the sky (not a single missing unit-conversion constant); raw DoLP error averages **~0.19–0.21** (worse than the Gal et al. benchmark the project's own docs already list as *failing* and exclude from the default gate); raw AoP error averages **~44°**, on a variable whose maximum possible error is 90° — consistent with near-random. This is the real, current state of the flagship physics test, and it is not resolved.
3. **The general validation suite's result artifacts do not exist on disk and have never been tracked in git.** `monte_carlo_cpp/results/` is fully gitignored, and `monte_carlo_cpp/results/validation/` — which would contain `validation_report.json` and the Zawada/DISORT/IPRT benchmark smoke-test outputs repeatedly cited by exact number in `STATUS_README.md`, `MODEL_ASSUMPTIONS.md`, and `monte_carlo_cpp/README.md` — is absent from the filesystem and has zero git history. The benchmark *input* data and *comparison code* are real and present; the claimed *pass results* are currently unverifiable from any artifact in the repository.
4. **There is no formal, peer-reviewed "Marseille paper" to be faithful to.** The cited "Marseille paper" is an unpublished dataset release (Poughon et al. 2024, *"A 2 month-long annotated skylight polarization images database,"* `doi.org/10.57745/9L2YUB`, marked "VERSION PROVISOIRE," whose associated publication is listed as "To come (future publication in BMC Research Notes)"). Its own authors flag their bundled reference Rayleigh simulator as inexact and recommend a different tool (OpenSky) for rigorous comparison — a recommendation this project does not follow, and does not discuss. "Fidelity to the Marseille paper" is therefore not a well-posed question in the form the audit brief assumes; the better-posed question (does the project correctly use the dataset's own calibration files) has a genuinely positive partial answer.
5. **What was directly verified as correct is real and should not be discounted**: the Rayleigh phase function, Rayleigh polarization fraction, Rayleigh/aerosol Mueller-matrix formalism, and — most importantly — the scattering-plane basis-rotation logic (`MonteCarloDriver.cpp:775-826`, `eventMuellerMatrix`) that most polarized Monte Carlo codes get wrong, all match standard textbook conventions on direct inspection. The `strict_paper_mode` guard against ad hoc empirical twilight fudge factors is genuinely enforced in code, not just claimed in prose (`MonteCarloDriver.cpp:3717-3720`). Five low-level executable physics unit tests genuinely pass, with a real, dated CTest log.

The honest state of this project is: **a well-built solver with unproven real-world accuracy.** The core question is not "what paper do we write" — it is "why is the raw model nine orders of magnitude off on intensity and effectively uncorrelated in AoP on the one real dataset tested, and does the model actually pass the benchmarks it claims to pass." Both are answerable, bounded pieces of technical work, not narrative/framing decisions.

## 3. Intended Scientific and Computational Workflow

There is **no notebook that constitutes "the realistic implementation."** This is an important scoping correction to the audit's starting premise, established by direct inspection (`notes.ipynb` cell-by-cell scan; all 5 notebooks characterized in §2). The realistic implementation is a standalone C++ command-line application. The actual workflow is:

1. **Inputs** are assembled by Python tooling (`monte_carlo_cpp/tools/build_paper_case_inputs.py`, `generate_gas_cross_sections.py`, `generate_aerosol_reference.py`) into CSV/JSON tables: a 1D spherically-stratified atmosphere profile, Rayleigh/O2/O3/O4/H2O/NO2 cross-section tables, an aerosol phase/Mueller lookup table, a solar spectrum, and (for the Marseille case) case-local AERONET-derived aerosol optics and an instrument-response proxy.
2. **The C++ solver** (`monte_carlo_cpp/src/MonteCarloDriver.cpp`, entry point `runMonteCarloSimulation`) is a *backward* Monte Carlo estimator from observer-centered fisheye sky bins: single scatter is handled by a deterministic line-integral control variate, second scatter by a deterministic adaptive-quadrature control variate, and third-and-higher orders by a stochastic, survival-biased, source-guided path tracer with median-of-means variance reduction (`RobustHigherOrderGroups`, `MonteCarloDriver.cpp:115-217`).
3. **Output** is a `SkyResult` (Stokes I/Q/U/V per sky bin, order-resolved) written to CSV/JSON.
4. **Validation** runs through two separate executables: `ValidationRunner` (general convergence + external-benchmark + measurement gates, `ValidationQA.cpp`) and `MeasurementCaseRunner` (exact per-direction published-case scoring, `measurement_case_main.cpp`), the latter used for the Marseille case because the former's full run is currently too slow to complete on this cluster.
5. **The Marseille case specifically** additionally applies a row-wise post-hoc calibration (`measurement_model_quality_calibration.csv`) before the gate is evaluated — this is the step this audit finds to be circular (§9, Finding C-1).
6. **Python (`spherical/`) is downstream-only**: it reads the C++ outputs and produces comparison plots. It is correctly documented as such and was not treated as a primary scientific artifact in this audit.
7. **`paper/`** is a separate export/packaging layer that mirrors frozen artifacts with checksums (`MANIFEST.csv`) and manuscript-ready text (`text_snippets.md`), explicitly without re-running the solver.

## 5. Physics and Mathematical Assessment

**Core scattering kernels (`PhaseFunctions.cpp`, `Polarization.cpp`): correct.** Every formula checked against standard references (Chandrasekhar; Hansen & Travis 1974; Mishchenko, Travis & Lacis) matched: the Rayleigh phase function is correctly normalized (hand-verified to integrate to 1 over 4π sr); the Rayleigh polarization fraction and Mueller matrix use the standard non-depolarizing form (`f22=f11`, `f33=f44`, `f34=0`); the rejection-sampling implementation of the Rayleigh angular distribution (`sampleRayleighDirection`, `PhaseFunctions.cpp:336-347`) is a textbook-correct rejection sampler (envelope `M=0.75` matches the true peak of the `(3/8)(1+cos²θ)` marginal, verified by direct calculation); DoLP and AoP are defined consistently (`DoLP=√(Q²+U²)/I`, `AoP=0.5·atan2(U,Q)`) and used identically everywhere this convention appears across the codebase, including in the otherwise-problematic calibration tooling — internal convention consistency is genuinely good.

**Scattering-plane basis rotation: correct and non-trivial.** `eventMuellerMatrix` (`MonteCarloDriver.cpp:799-826`) implements the standard `R(χ_out)·M_scatter·R(−χ_in)` procedure for propagating Stokes parameters across changing reference frames at each scattering event, with a physically sensible local-vertical reference direction (`referenceAxisForRay`, line 775) that degrades gracefully at poles. This is the single most common source of sign/convention bugs in polarized Monte Carlo codes, and it appears to be implemented correctly. This is consistent with (though does not, by itself, prove) the project's claim that spherical-vector benchmarks pass.

**Gas/aerosol optics (`WavelengthHandling.cpp`): correct structure.** Extinction is correctly assembled as the sum of Rayleigh + aerosol scattering/absorption + O3/O2/O4/H2O/NO2 absorption; single-scattering albedo is correctly restricted to the scattering fraction; O4 absorption correctly scales as the square of O2 number density (`line 261`), consistent with O4 being a collision-induced complex — and the previously-documented cm⁵/m⁵ unit bug (a 10¹⁰ error) now has a regression guard (`tests/test_wavelength.cpp:52-53`, confirmed present).

**Surface reflection (`SurfaceReflection.cpp`): correct and moot for the flagship case.** Fresnel and Cox-Munk formulas are standard. However, `directSolarRadiance` only fires when `muIn>0` (sun above the local horizontal) — for the frozen Marseille case the sun is *below* the horizon by construction, so surface choice is a structural no-op. The project's own surface-sensitivity check confirms this (`max_abs_delta=0.0`). This means the surface model's correctness is currently untested by the one real case the project cares about, which is an honest and correctly-diagnosed limitation, not a hidden one.

**What is not physically resolved:**

- **Missing atmospheric refraction** (§4 table) in exactly the grazing/below-horizon geometry where refraction is known to matter most for twilight. No test or discussion addresses whether this is negligible for the intended science case.
- **The raw Marseille mismatch is large, structured (not simply a missing constant), and currently without a stated root cause.** The required per-point intensity gain spans `4.29e8` to `2.30e10` (a ~54× range) with only weak correlation to viewing geometry (`corr(log₁₀gain, zenith)=0.245`, `corr(log₁₀gain, azimuth)=0.280` — recomputed during this audit from `measurement_model_quality_calibration.csv`). A pure units/radiometric-calibration mismatch between camera DN and physical radiance would produce a *single*, geometry-independent constant, not a 54×-varying, weakly-geometry-correlated factor. This points to a genuine unresolved shape/pattern mismatch between the modeled and observed sky, not merely an absolute-scale issue.
- **Ad hoc, non-physical tuning knobs exist in the transport code** (`boostUnpolarizedIntensity`, `scalePolarization`, and the `twilight_*_boost`/`twilight_*_scale`/`twilight_order_depolarization` config fields; `MonteCarloDriver.cpp:1355-1414`). `boostUnpolarizedIntensity` multiplies only intensity, mechanically deflating DoLP as a side effect — a fit-the-answer mechanism, not a physical process. **Positive finding, verified in code**: `strict_paper_mode=true` genuinely rejects any config where these are non-neutral (`MonteCarloDriver.cpp:3717-3720`, confirmed to throw), and the actual config used for the frozen headline run (`_batched_work/..._robust_r2__shard_002__batch_0062_0062.cfg`) does not set any of them, so they default to neutral (`MonteCarloDriver.hpp:110-114`, confirmed `false`/`1.0`). The raw numbers cited above are genuinely un-fudged. The mere existence of these knobs, however, is informative context: at some point in this project's history the raw mismatch was severe enough that hand-tuned per-regime correction factors were built as a response, before being set aside for the paper path.

## 6. Numerical and Computational Assessment

**Genuine strengths:** deterministic single- and second-scatter control variates (a legitimate, standard variance-reduction technique) reduce reliance on pure analog Monte Carlo for the dominant contributions; adaptive optical-depth/line-integral step sizing replaces an earlier fixed-500m brute-force walk with altitude-shell-crossing integration (a real, verified algorithmic improvement, not just a config change — confirmed in `MonteCarloDriver.cpp` and corroborated by an ~order-of-magnitude runtime drop recorded in `profile_subset_runtime_probe.log`); Russian roulette and survival biasing are standard and correctly gated; RNG state is seeded deterministically per direction/sample/band and is serializable for checkpoint/resume (`serializeRngState`/`restoreRngState`, `MonteCarloDriver.cpp:1891-1911`), which is good practice for reproducibility.

**The higher-order estimator is a median-of-16-group-means, not a sample mean**, and this choice does not appear to be cross-validated. Direct inspection of a representative checkpoint (`_batched_work/..._robust_r2__shard_001__batch_0246_0246_checkpoint.txt`) shows 256 higher-order samples split evenly into 16 groups of 16 each, with the final per-direction estimate being the **median of those 16 group means** (`RobustHigherOrderGroups::medianOfMeans`, `MonteCarloDriver.cpp:182-216`), not their overall mean. Median-of-means is a legitimate robust-statistics technique for heavy-tailed estimators, but it is a *biased* estimator of the mean in general, and this audit found no comparison anywhere in the project against a plain running mean to check whether that bias is material for this specific transport problem. Given the group sums shown in the sampled checkpoint vary by roughly four orders of magnitude across the 16 groups (e.g. group 6 sums to ~1e-13 while group 15 sums to ~5e-6 for the I component), the underlying per-sample distribution is visibly heavy-tailed — exactly the regime where median-of-means and mean can diverge materially. This is a **risk, not a proven defect**: it may be a sound choice, but it is currently an unvalidated one.

**Runtime history is a legitimate concern in its own right.** `MARSEILLE_JOB_FAILURE_REPORT_2026-04-17.md` documents, with primary Slurm-log evidence, that a single higher-order sample did not complete within 72 hours under the original strict/profile settings. The eventual fix combined real algorithmic speedups (verified above) with an estimator restructuring (branch cap, robust grouping) whose numerical adequacy at the specific settings used for the frozen headline run has not been demonstrated via an explicit convergence study *at those settings*. The project's one checked-in convergence gate (`convergence_peak_intensity_rel ≤ 0.05`, etc.) compares half- vs. full-photon-budget runs of the **default clear-sky config**, not the strict Marseille paper configuration — and in any case its result (`results/validation/validation_report.json`) is not currently present on disk to inspect (§9, Finding V-1).

**No per-bin Monte Carlo uncertainty is reported alongside the raw model-vs-reference comparison.** Given the scale of the raw Marseille mismatch (orders of magnitude in intensity, tens of degrees in AoP), MC noise is very unlikely to be the explanation — but the general absence of reported statistical uncertainty is a gap that would matter for any case with a smaller, more marginal mismatch, and is worth closing before quantitative paper claims are made from this pipeline.

## 7. Software and Reproducibility Assessment

**Genuine strengths:** clear separation of concerns (C++ = "scientific source of truth," Python = "downstream plotting only" — this framing is stated in the docs and is actually true of the code, a rare instance of documentation matching implementation exactly); config-driven, reproducible runs; provenance JSONs for the Marseille case (`paper_case_provenance.json`, `measurement_reduction.json`) recording exact data sources (AERONET inversion timestamps, Open-Meteo fetch parameters); a genuine checksummed manifest (`paper/MANIFEST.csv`, SHA256 per artifact) for the publication package; carefully, consistently hedged manuscript language (`paper/CLAIMS.md`, `text_snippets.md` — every methods/results/caption paragraph states the calibration limitation).

**Confirmed defect: the general validation-suite result artifacts do not exist and were never version-controlled.** `monte_carlo_cpp/results/` is excluded wholesale by `.gitignore` (`# Generated run outputs` / `monte_carlo_cpp/results/`), `git ls-files monte_carlo_cpp/results/` returns nothing, and `git log --all -- monte_carlo_cpp/results/validation` returns nothing — this path has never been tracked at any point in this repository's history. On the current filesystem, only `results/measurement_case_reports/`, `results/pbs`, and `results/slurm` exist; `results/validation/` (which would hold `validation_report.json` and the benchmark smoke-test outputs) is simply absent. This was checked three independent ways during this audit (directory scan, direct `ls`, git history) and consistently confirmed.

**Confirmed (positive): the low-level unit-style physics tests genuinely pass, with a real dated log**, resolving an apparent contradiction along the way. `LastTestsFailed.log` (timestamp 09:43:52) lists all 5 tests as failed, which at first reading looks alarming — but `LastTest.log` and `CTestCostData.txt` (both timestamped 09:47:57, four minutes later, same day as the final commit) show all 5 tests (`phasefunctions`, `surface`, `polarization`, `atmosphere`, `wavelength`) individually marked "Test Passed." with real per-test timings. `LastTestsFailed.log` is a stale leftover from an earlier same-morning run and is superseded by the later, timestamp-confirmed clean run. This is reported in full because the audit brief asked for genuine due diligence, not because it changes the conclusion: the unit tests did pass.

**Confirmed defect: physics-critical bug fixes are not traceable via git history.** The documented O4 cm⁵/m⁵ unit bug (a 10¹⁰ error, per `STATUS_README.md`) has no commit with "O4," "units," or similar in its message (`git log --all --grep` returns nothing on those terms); the one plausible candidate commit (`5e0c7a8 "bug fixes"`) touches only checkpoint/batching/Slurm files, not the wavelength-handling or cross-section-generation code. The fix is real and currently guarded by a test (confirmed above), but its history is only recoverable from prose descriptions in markdown files, which is a real (if minor-to-moderate) provenance gap for a scientifically consequential fix.

**Minor, pervasive defect: most in-repo documentation links are stale Windows paths.** The overwhelming majority of file links in `STATUS_README.md`, `MODEL_ASSUMPTIONS.md`, and `monte_carlo_cpp/README.md` point to `/c:/Users/ashton/Desktop/projects/light-scattering/...`, while the project now runs on a Linux cluster at `/work/vmo703/light-scattering/...` (as seen in the more recently added Marseille-specific documents, which correctly use the Linux path). This means most cross-references in the two primary status documents are currently broken on the environment where the project actually runs. Cosmetic, but pervasive enough to note, and symptomatic of documentation not being fully carried forward through the Windows→Linux/cluster transition.

## 9. Defects, Risks, and Open Questions

### Critical

**C-1. The flagship Marseille validation metric is a mathematical tautology with zero power to detect an incorrect model.**
- Evidence: `build_measurement_model_calibration.py:88` (`intensity_gain = reference_intensity / model_intensity`), `:92` (`dolp_scale = reference_dop / model_dop`), `:100` (`aop_offset_deg = wrap_half_turn_deg(reference_aop - model_aop)`) — three free parameters solved independently per sky direction to force exact agreement; `ValidationQA.cpp:317-334` applies exactly the inverse transform to the same points. Verified during this audit via a fresh self-consistency check across all 683 rows of `measurement_model_quality_calibration.csv`: zero mismatches, using the recomputed offset formula directly from the file's own columns.
- Why it matters: the number reported to referees/collaborators as "the Marseille result" (`normalized_rmse=5.45e-18`, etc.) would be identical in magnitude whether the underlying model were perfect, uniformly wrong, or pure noise, because the calibration has exactly as many free parameters as it has points to fit.
- Likely effect on results: any paper text, table, or figure that presents these numbers as evidence of model accuracy (rather than pipeline plumbing) would be presenting a false claim, even though the project's own `CLAIMS.md` explicitly tries to guard against this with hedged language.
- Status: **proven**, not suspected — verified by reading both the derivation and application code and by independent recomputation.
- Recommended next action: do not report the calibrated numbers as evidence of anything beyond data-path/plumbing correctness (the project's own `CLAIMS.md` already gestures at this); design and execute a genuine holdout split (fit calibration on a subset of the 683 directions, score on the withheld remainder) before any physical claim is attached to the Marseille comparison.

### Major

**M-1. The raw (uncalibrated) Marseille mismatch is large and has no stated root cause.**
- Evidence: recomputed from `measurement_model_quality_calibration.csv`: intensity ratio spans `4.29e8`–`2.30e10` (mean log₁₀ ratio 9.40, stdev 0.25 — a ~50× spread, not a single constant); raw DoLP mean absolute error ≈ 0.19–0.21; raw AoP mean error ≈ 44° (max possible 90°); weak correlation of required correction with zenith/azimuth (r ≈ 0.24–0.28 for intensity, ≈ 0.01/–0.16 for AoP).
- Why it matters: this is the actual, real-world accuracy of the physics on the one dataset that matters for this project's stated goal, and it is currently unaddressed by anything beyond a calibration that hides it.
- Likely effect on results: no current claim about twilight polarization physical accuracy can be supported.
- Status: proven (the numbers are directly computed from the project's own artifact); root cause is an open question, not established.
- Recommended next action: treat this as the primary open scientific/technical problem. Candidate hypotheses worth testing in order of cheapness: (a) absolute radiometric units/normalization between model output and camera DN — check for a single missing constant vs. residual pattern after removing one; (b) missing atmospheric refraction in the below-horizon geometry (§5); (c) case-local aerosol/atmosphere input quality; (d) the Marseille sensor-frame Q/U rotation/reduction itself.

**M-2. General benchmark/convergence validation is currently unverifiable from any artifact in the repository.**
- Evidence: `monte_carlo_cpp/results/validation/` absent from disk (confirmed via directory scan, direct `ls`, and `git log --all` on that path returning nothing); `.gitignore` excludes `monte_carlo_cpp/results/` wholesale.
- Why it matters: the strongest non-circular evidence this project claims to have (the Zawada spherical-vector benchmarks, DISORT/IPRT comparisons, convergence gate) cannot currently be checked by anyone, including this audit.
- Likely effect on results: unknown until rerun — the underlying code and physics look sound (§5–6), so this may well resolve favorably, but "looks sound" is not the same as "verified," and the audit brief is explicit that it should not be treated as such.
- Status: proven as a *documentation/reproducibility* gap; the underlying benchmark pass/fail claims themselves are an unverified assumption pending rerun.
- Recommended next action: rerun `ValidationRunner` and the Zawada/DISORT/IPRT/Rozenberg/Koomen comparisons, and commit or otherwise durably preserve the resulting artifacts (even a deliberately-tracked exception to the `results/` gitignore rule, or an artifact archive alongside `paper/tables/reproducibility/` in the style already used for the Marseille case).

**M-3. The higher-order Monte Carlo estimator uses an unvalidated median-of-means construction.**
- Evidence: `RobustHigherOrderGroups::medianOfMeans` (`MonteCarloDriver.cpp:182-216`); a sampled checkpoint shows 16 groups of 16 samples each with group sums varying over ~4 orders of magnitude, consistent with a heavy-tailed per-sample distribution where median-of-means and mean can diverge.
- Why it matters: if this estimator is materially biased relative to a plain mean for this transport problem, the deterministic-plus-higher-order sky field used for both the Marseille comparison and any future production run would be systematically off in a way not captured by the existing convergence gate (which, per M-2, cannot currently be checked anyway).
- Status: a risk, not a proven defect — no comparison against a plain-mean estimator was found anywhere in the project.
- Recommended next action: for at least a few representative directions, compute both the plain mean and the median-of-means over the same higher-order samples and compare; if they diverge materially, either increase samples-per-group or revisit the estimator.

### Moderate

**Mo-1. Ad hoc, non-physical twilight tuning knobs exist in the transport code**, gated off correctly for the paper path (verified: `strict_paper_mode` enforcement is real, `MonteCarloDriver.cpp:3717-3720`) but present as evidence that the raw mismatch was, at some point, severe enough to prompt fudge-factor patches rather than a diagnosed fix. Not currently active in the frozen run; worth removing entirely once the real root cause (M-1) is found, so they cannot be silently re-enabled later.

**Mo-2. No atmospheric refraction model**, in exactly the geometric regime (grazing/below-horizon twilight) where it is most likely to matter. Not tested for materiality either way.

**Mo-3. Physics-critical bug fixes (the O4 unit bug) are not traceable via git commit history**, only via prose descriptions in markdown status files, which is a weaker form of provenance than a dedicated, well-labeled commit or changelog entry.

**Mo-4. No per-bin Monte Carlo uncertainty reported** alongside model-vs-reference comparisons, making it structurally harder to distinguish "real mismatch" from "MC noise" for any future case where the gap is smaller than the (currently enormous) Marseille one.

### Minor

**M-min-1.** Most links in `STATUS_README.md` and `MODEL_ASSUMPTIONS.md` use stale Windows paths (`/c:/Users/ashton/...`) rather than the current Linux path (`/work/vmo703/...`); purely cosmetic/navigational, does not affect any result.

**M-min-2.** Commit messages are frequently too coarse ("bug fixes," "minor changes," "moving project arc") to independently trace which specific scientific change happened in which commit without cross-referencing prose docs.

## 11. Paper-Readiness Assessment

**Currently, reliably supportable:** essentially nothing that depends on the Marseille comparison. The raw comparison fails; the calibrated comparison is not evidence.

**Potentially supportable, pending M-2 (regenerate and confirm benchmark artifacts):** a methods/software paper — "an open, spherically-stratified, polarized Monte Carlo twilight radiative-transfer solver with deterministic control variates, validated against published scalar and vector radiative-transfer benchmarks." This requires: (1) rerunning `ValidationRunner` and the Zawada/DISORT/IPRT comparisons and confirming the previously-claimed numbers still hold, with the resulting artifacts preserved; (2) resolving or explicitly bounding the median-of-means estimator question (Finding M-3); (3) an honest treatment of the Marseille case as a *negative* or *open* result rather than a validated one, or omitting it from the paper's evidentiary claims entirely.

**Not currently supportable:** any claim of quantitative twilight-sky polarization accuracy against real-world measurements; any reproduction/validation study built around the Marseille dataset; any physical-interpretation paper about below-horizon twilight polarization (the below-horizon regime is specifically where the raw model is worst and where refraction, the most obvious missing physical ingredient, is absent).

**What each direction would additionally need:**
- *Methods/software paper*: regenerated benchmark artifacts (M-2); a stated, defensible position on the median-of-means estimator (M-3); no reliance on the Marseille numbers as validation.
- *Marseille validation/reproduction paper*: a genuine holdout-validated calibration (or, better, a diagnosed and fixed raw model that closes without calibration); resolution of the ~9-order-of-magnitude raw intensity puzzle; a stated treatment of refraction.
- *Parameter-study or physical-interpretation paper*: none of the necessary sweep work has been done yet (only one surface-model sensitivity check exists, with a null/structural result); this would require new simulation campaigns beyond what currently exists.

## 13. Final Verdict

**Implementation is not yet ready; substantial technical and scientific validation work remains.**

Direct answers to the required questions:

- **Is the implementation correct?** Partially. The core scattering/polarization physics kernels are correct on direct inspection. The full system's output on the one real-world dataset tested is not yet understood — a large, structured, unexplained mismatch remains.
- **Is it faithful to the Marseille paper?** There is no peer-reviewed Marseille paper to be faithful to; it is an unpublished dataset with its associated methods publication listed as "to come." The project does correctly use the dataset's own geometric calibration files, but departs undocumented from the dataset authors' own recommended reference simulator.
- **Is it sufficiently validated?** No. The flagship validation is proven circular. The strongest claimed independent evidence (published spherical/vector benchmarks) is currently unverifiable from any artifact in the repository. The only artifact-backed, physically-informative results are negative.
- **Can its current outputs be trusted?** The calibrated Marseille outputs: no, not as physics — they are mathematically forced to agree with the reference regardless of model quality. The raw model outputs: not yet, given the scale of the demonstrated real-world mismatch. The core physics kernels, based on direct code inspection: yes, provisionally, pending confirmation of the missing benchmark artifacts.
- **Could a paper be started now?** A narrow methods/software paper could be *started*, contingent on regenerating and confirming the general benchmark evidence first. A Marseille-validation or twilight-physics paper could not.
- **What is the strongest paper that could be written from the work in its present state?** A software/methods paper describing the solver's architecture, its deterministic control-variate estimator, and its (once reconfirmed) performance against published scalar and vector radiative-transfer benchmarks — explicitly not resting on the Marseille comparison for its evidentiary claims.
- **Is "what are we actually doing with this now" truly the only major remaining question?** No.
- **If not, what other blocking questions remain?** (1) Why is the raw model ~9 orders of magnitude off in intensity and ~44° off in AoP against the one real dataset tested — and is that a units bug, missing refraction physics, an input-data problem, or something else? (2) Do the general external benchmarks this project's credibility partly rests on actually still pass — they need to be rerun and the results preserved before anyone can know. (3) Is the higher-order Monte Carlo estimator's median-of-means construction materially biased? (4) What would a genuine holdout validation of the Marseille calibration actually show — does the pipeline have any real predictive skill once the tautology is removed?

These are bounded, answerable technical and scientific questions, not framing or narrative decisions. Resolving them is the actual remaining work.
